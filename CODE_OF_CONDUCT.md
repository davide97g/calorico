# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Disagree about code, not about each other.

## What is expected

- Assume the person on the other side is acting in good faith and is busy.
- Criticise the change, not the author. "This breaks X" is useful; "this is
  amateur work" is not.
- Accept that a maintained-in-spare-time project moves slowly, and that "no" is
  a legitimate answer to a feature request.
- Remember what this project is about. Calorie tracking touches body weight and
  eating, subjects on which people carry real weight of their own. Keep comments
  about bodies, diets and eating habits out of issues and reviews entirely.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Comments demeaning someone's body, weight, eating, gender, sexuality, race,
  religion, nationality, age or disability.
- Unsolicited advice about anyone's diet or body, whether framed as concern or
  as a joke.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwanted attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately wasting maintainer time.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, code
comments, and anywhere someone is representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Reports go to **ghiotto.davidenko@gmail.com**. They are read by the maintainer
alone and kept confidential.

Expect a reply within a week. Depending on what happened, the response ranges
from a private word, through editing or removing content, to a temporary or
permanent block from the repository. The maintainer decides, and does not owe a
public accounting of it.

If the person you need to report is the maintainer, GitHub Support can act on
conduct in a repository independently: <https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse>.
